# v1.11 — drawn set pieces, depth charts and development arrows

## Penalty and free-kick mini-game

In interactive Watch Match, your penalties and free kicks now open a draw-to-aim mini-game directly. The previous three-option menus are replaced for these two situations. The other four key-moment types retain their tactical options. Both the real taker's and opposing goalkeeper's persistent illustrated portraits are displayed, including a goalkeeper on the goal scene.

Draw from the ball toward any part of the goal, preview your stroke, optionally Redraw, then Take shot. Straight and curved paths are retained. A tap gives a straight aim; keyboard users can focus the pitch, move a target with arrow keys and press Enter to preview. A fixed footer keeps the shot controls accessible on small phones. Pointer capture, cancellation, SVG coordinate transforms and a 64-point bound protect touch input without scrolling the aiming area accidentally.

The ball animation follows the same saved polyline as the preview. A defensive-wall block stops it at a point on that line; a save animates the keeper toward the endpoint. Reduced-motion users see the final position directly. The match clock holds until the result is continued. The result and path save immediately, so refreshing during flight replays the same attempt without rerolling it.

Your target, distance from the keeper, tightness to the frame and stroke complexity affect scoring probabilities, alongside the existing individual taker skills, goalkeeper skills, fatigue and form. An endpoint outside the goal misses. Inside the goal, the attempt can score or be saved; a free kick through the wall can be blocked. A curve around the wall avoids its interception risk. Corner placement never guarantees a goal. These are original game mechanics, not claims about real-world conversion statistics.

Only the current interactive set piece can accept a stroke. Once selected, the shot cannot be replaced to try for a different result. Scores, saves, assists, match ratings and awards use the real event ledger. Blocked attempts do not create a phantom goal or save. Later substitutions preserve completed drawn shots. Automatic watching and Quick Sim remain available without the mini-game; historic selections and older saves remain valid.

## Positional depth chart

Open **Squad → Depth**. All eleven starting spots are shown with their role, current player and effective positional rating. Choose a spot to edit its depth:

- Slide through candidates, ordered by effective positional rating, to change the actual starter.
- Tap Start beside a candidate, or drag their grip into the starting spot using touch, pen or mouse.
- The drop target stays visible while scrolling the candidate list. Tapping a candidate's identity opens their profile.

Selecting an existing starter swaps positions. Selecting a rotation player puts the previous starter in rotation. The existing authoritative assignment rules preserve eleven unique players, goalkeeper restrictions, registration, injury eligibility, designated-star validity and position penalties. These changes affect the same XI used by Matchday; they save normally. During a pending matchweek, lineup actions lock and direct the user to the existing Matchday substitution controls.

## Development and decline arrows

Small arrows appear beside player names in the depth chart, squad list, pitch/selection view, Progress and profile:

| Arrows | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Green ↑ | Normal development, still improving |
| Green ↑↑ | Fast development |
| Green ↑↑↑ | Ultra development |
| Red ↓ | Normal decline |
| Red ↓↓ | Fast decline |
| Red ↓↓↓ | Ultra decline |
| Muted dash | Stable / no current growth |

The indicator calls a read-only projection of the existing development engine. It uses recent minutes (or the current XI before any matches), age, form, natural-role suitability, coach quality, manager specialty, injury state, individual growth tendency/rate and potential headroom. It measures fractional changes in role-weighted primary skills, so it can show improving ability before a rounded OVR increases. The projection does not advance a week, trigger a real injury or mutate saved data.

Growth levels use projected season rates of +1 for fast and +3 for ultra; decline uses −0.75 for fast and −1.5 for ultra. The legend explains both. Tooltips and accessible text label estimates; future playing time, injuries, form and coaching can change them. Memoization avoids recomputing these projections on every playback minute or drag movement.
